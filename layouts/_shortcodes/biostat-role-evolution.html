Workforce signal

Growing demand for quantitative and data-science expertise

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics projects that employment of statisticians will grow by approximately 8.5% between 2024 and 2034, while employment of data scientists will grow by approximately 33.5%. Both projections exceed the approximately 3.1% growth expected across the overall U.S. economy during the same period. Statisticians and data scientists are distinct occupational categories, but these projections together indicate increasing demand for professionals who can integrate statistical reasoning, computation, domain knowledge, and responsible evaluation. See the BLS occupational projections and overall employment outlook.

This workforce transition is especially relevant to health research because the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence creates a need for expertise beyond model development. Clinical and biomedical organizations increasingly need professionals who can determine whether AI evidence is valid, reproducible, transportable across populations and settings, clinically useful, and suitable for responsible real-world adoption.

Federal direction reinforces the need for evaluation expertise

The NIH Strategic Plan for Data Science, 2025–2030 calls for strengthening a broad data-science workforce and developing resources that support rigorous AI assessment, validation, and adoption. This direction places evaluation, reproducibility, responsible use, and workforce development alongside continued investment in new computational methods and AI technologies.

The FDA's 2025 draft lifecycle guidance for AI-enabled medical devices similarly emphasizes that evaluation cannot end when a model is developed. Transparency, bias, documentation, risk management, and ongoing performance monitoring must be considered across the total product lifecycle. These priorities create an important role for biostatisticians and quantitative health scientists who can connect statistical validity with clinical context, regulatory expectations, and real-world evidence.
