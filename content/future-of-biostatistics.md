        essential, while the scope of responsibility is expanding across the full

        real-world monitoring and responsible adoption.

        {{< biostat-role-evolution >}}

        The emerging opportunity is to combine statistical reasoning, computation,
        clinical context, and responsible evidence appraisal. In this expanded role,
        biostatisticians help determine not only whether an analysis is technically
        correct, but whether a Clinical AI system is valid, reproducible,
        transportable, clinically useful, and appropriate for the populations and
        settings in which it will be used.

      subtitle: 'Foundation → Understanding → Evaluation → Research'
      text: |-
        The Clinical AI Evaluation pathway is designed around a staged progression:

        1. **Quantitative Foundation** — biostatistics, study design, uncertainty,
           and evidence-based reasoning.
        2. **Understanding Clinical AI** — AI and machine-learning concepts,
           clinical applications, data limitations, and responsible AI literacy.
        3. **Clinical AI Evaluation** — validation, calibration, transportability,
           subgroup performance, fairness, reproducibility, and clinical utility.
        4. **Mentored Research Experience** — applied evaluation using real clinical
           data, reproducible analysis, and scholarly dissemination.

        This pathway does not train learners only to build new models. It prepares
        quantitative health scientists to evaluate the validity and trustworthiness
        of AI evidence and to support responsible real-world adoption. It also
        extends the traditional biostatistics identity from **analysis producer** to
        **steward of evidence quality and Clinical AI validity**.
